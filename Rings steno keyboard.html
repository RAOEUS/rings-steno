A steno board for two thumbs

Each thumb has one stick. It opens wherever that thumb lands on its half, so there is nothing to aim at. The directions are the board itself: push toward where the key sits. Keys add up until the stroke is sent, a moment after both thumbs are off the glass. That is the only clock: how long a thumb rests never changes what its push means.

push
Your hand's block of six, as on the board: T P H over K W R on the left, -F -P -L over -R -B -G on the right. Push toward a key and lift. Push again for another key from the block.
tap, then push
A tap sends your next push to the keys around that block, where the board has them. Left: the number bar above, S- to the left, * to the right, A and O underneath. Right: -T and -D above, -S left and -Z right, E and U underneath. Straight down is both vowels. Two ticks tell you the tap landed. After that push the stick is back on the block. Any touch that lifts without a push is a tap, however long it rests.
tap twice
The stick's click: S- on the left, * on the right. Two taps on the right undo the last stroke. A longer tick tells you each stroke has gone and the next can start.

More than one key from the block

one by one
Push, lift, push again. Each push adds its key, so the rolls below are shortcuts you can leave for later.
neighbours
Roll from one key onto the next and you get both. The same goes for the keys around the block: -T rolled across to -D, A down round to O.
skip
Roll across three or more. Forward in steno order, say T round to H, keeps every key. Backward, H round to T, keeps only the two ends.
drop one
After a roll, back up one step. The key you stop on is dropped: T, P, H, then back to P, is TH.
or two trips
Without lifting, come back to the centre and push out again.

Arpeggiate, for learning

Switch it on in Settings and the clock stops. Each push adds its keys and they stay down while you find the next one. A push goes to the block, a tap and then a push goes to the keys around it, and pushing a key that is already down takes it out. Back undoes your last push and Clear empties the stroke, so a slip never has to be sent. Press Send when the stroke is right, the way the space bar does in Plover's arpeggiate. Two taps are the sticks' clicks as usual: S- on the left, * on the right, so two right taps and Send undoes the last stroke.

The block sticks are plover_controller's, with every path of its default mapping unchanged. KAT is a push down-left for K, a tap and another push down-left for A, and on the right a tap and a push up-left for -T. The tape shows the stroke you are building in red.

What works like Plover

Translation, undo, suffix folding, spelling rules, modes, retro commands, modal dictionaries, stitching and the Lapwing number key behave as in Plover: this engine was checked against Plover itself on tens of thousands of stroke sequences. Key commands such as arrows, Return and BackSpace act on the text on this page. The four Python dictionaries that come with Lapwing run as built-in ports, checked against the originals on every possible stroke. Any other .py file runs in a Python runtime that downloads the first time it is needed.

The engine is a JavaScript port of the translation and formatting code of Plover by the Open Steno Project, with the behaviour of the modal-dictionary, stitching and last-translation plugins, and is offered under the same GPL-2.0-or-later licence. The stick paths are the default mapping of plover_controller by Tadeo Kondrak and contributors (GPL-2.0). Word dictionaries are never bundled: they come from the files you add, and stay in this browser. The built-in ports carry the tables of jeff-phrasing by Jeff Wu, emily-symbols and emily-modifiers by EPLHREU, and abby-left-hand-modifiers by Abkwreu, as packaged in plover-lapwing-aio.
